## 1. What blocks the first benign experiment

The smallest **proposed next activity** is a manual, narrowly bounded benign capture-feasibility probe only after a separate written authorization:

- Two isolated VirtualBox guests: generator sends a harmless identifiable request to an operator-owned observer endpoint; observer captures request and response directions. This is endpoint observation, not a third-party passive-sensor claim.
- Inventory first: Windows edition/build, host/guest versions, VirtualBox, VM NICs, routes, backend/driver/tool versions, runtime account, capture privileges, storage, clock sources, and interface identifiers. Installation or driver changes require separate approval and are not authorized here.
- Choose host-only versus internal networking by owner decision. No NAT or bridged test NICs; no external routes; allowlisted endpoints only. `allow-vms` is conditional, not a requirement; promiscuous mode does not create switch visibility.
- Owner-approved rate, duration, volume, storage, retention, payload-handling, and stop limits are **TBD**. Missing limits block execution. Independent stop must remain available.
- Retain source request/response records and controlled payload handling decision; readable pcapng (or approved equivalent), interface, IP/ports/protocol/direction/marker matching, raw timestamps and clock quality, and capture/drop-counter availability. “Unavailable” is not zero; zero is not proof of no loss.
- Distinguish wrong interface, denied permission, no delivery, idle, backend failure, and expected-marker absence only within approved safe checks. Record pass, fail, or inconclusive criteria; unknown tolerances remain pending.

### 1.1 Proposed acceptance table for the probe (not executed)

The following table is an objective decision aid for the proposed probe. It is not an authorization, test result, or security-control verification. `REQ_MARKER` and `RESP_MARKER` mean predeclared benign identifiers selected in the approved activity record; no value is selected here. A missing prerequisite blocks execution rather than becoming an inconclusive pass.

| ID / check | Prerequisites and expected evidence | Pass outcome | Fail outcome | Inconclusive outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CF-01` Authorization and setup | Written activity authorization; two isolated VMs; operator-owned observer endpoint; approved host-only or internal network; no NAT/bridged test NICs or external routes; allowlisted endpoints; approved rate, duration, volume, storage, retention, payload handling, and independent-stop limits; Windows/VirtualBox/backend/account/interface inventory recorded. | All prerequisites are present, internally consistent, and reviewed before any probe activity. | Any required prerequisite is absent, disputed, or an unapproved route/endpoint is present; do not start, or if discovered after setup, stop before sending the request. | Inventory or authority records conflict and cannot be resolved by the authorized reviewer without changing scope. |
| `CF-02` Request/response markers | Generator source record contains the predeclared `REQ_MARKER`; observer application record contains the predeclared `RESP_MARKER`; the approved exchange plan and any retries are recorded; markers are identifiers only and are not model features. | Recorded marker counts match the approved exchange plan and source/observer records agree on the request and response. | The application exchange is known to have occurred but a required marker is missing, altered, duplicated beyond the approved plan, or associated with the wrong endpoint/port/protocol. | Source or observer records are incomplete, or retries/marker counts cannot be reconciled without assuming what happened. |
| `CF-03` Direction and endpoint matching | Capture metadata identifies generator-to-observer request and observer-to-generator response, with approved source/destination addresses, ports, protocol, and selected interface. | Both directions are present and match the authorized endpoint and protocol record. | The application records a completed exchange but a required direction is absent, reversed, on the wrong interface, or addressed outside the approved scope. | Direction or endpoint identity cannot be established because capture metadata or independent application records are incomplete. |
| `CF-04` Raw timestamps and clock quality | Raw packet timestamps are retained with source, resolution, clock basis, and any available offset/uncertainty record; request/response ordering is assessed using the approved observation semantics. | Required timestamp fields exist, ordering is consistent with the source records, and any approved clock-quality/tolerance rule is satisfied. | Timestamps are missing/unreadable, ordering contradicts the known exchange without an approved explanation, or an observed clock anomaly invalidates the timing evidence. | Clock source/quality, ordering, or tolerance cannot be established; do not convert the result to a latency claim. |
| `CF-05` Capture readability and record integrity | Approved pcapng or equivalent is retained with interface/link-layer metadata, readable packet records, and a mapping from records to the expected markers. | The file opens with the approved reader and the records needed for marker, endpoint, direction, and timestamp checks are available. | The file is unreadable, corrupt, unexpectedly truncated, or lacks the records/metadata required for the probe decision. | The reader can open the file but cannot establish whether missing records reflect truncation, unsupported metadata, or no delivery. |
| `CF-06` Loss-counter interpretation | Record offered/application counts, captured/processed counts, and OS/driver/backend counters where available. If the policy requires zero, the relevant counters report zero. | A nonzero loss exceeds the approved tolerance, or a required counter/measurement is known to violate the approved clean-probe condition. | A counter is unavailable, its scope is unknown, or no approved tolerance exists; the loss conclusion is unknown, not zero. |
| `CF-07` Timeout and stop behavior | A timeout and stop condition are written into the activity authorization before execution; independent stop remains available; application completion and capture end times are recorded. | The approved request/response exchange completes before the timeout, or an authorized stop occurs after complete evidence is safely recorded. | The exchange does not complete before the approved timeout, the stop condition is triggered by a known safety/scope violation, or required evidence is not safely finalized. | No response is observed but the records cannot distinguish no delivery, wrong interface, denied permission, idle traffic, or backend failure. |
| `CF-08` Isolation and scope | Routes, VM adapters, endpoint allowlist, capture interface, storage path, and stop record are captured before/after the probe; no third-party or physical-LAN destination is in scope. | The observed communication is limited to the approved generator/observer path and the independent stop remains available. | Any off-scope route, destination, unapproved adapter, credential/payload exposure, or uncontrolled traffic is observed or cannot be stopped safely. | The available route/egress evidence is insufficient to determine whether the lab remained isolated; suspend the probe and seek review. |
| `CF-09` Overall disposition | All applicable `CF-01`–`CF-08` evidence is assembled with reviewer identity and no criterion silently omitted. | Every mandatory check passes, no check is fail or inconclusive, and the evidence package is complete. This permits human review only; it does not authorize the next stage. | Any explicit safety violation, known marker/direction/readability failure, known timeout failure, or loss beyond approved tolerance is recorded; no live/model claim follows. | Any mandatory result remains unknown or ambiguous, including unavailable loss/timing/route evidence; no live/model claim follows and human review is required. |

This probe does **not** authorize implementation, driver installation, attack testing, training, inference, DIG, response, security-control claims, or product demonstration. Passing permits human review only and does not authorize the next stage. Pktmon, if considered, is a different observation source and requires separate parity evidence.
